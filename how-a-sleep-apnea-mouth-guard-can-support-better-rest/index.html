How a Sleep Apnea Mouth Guard Can Support Better Rest

Published August 10, 2026By Dr. Rose Magno

Sleep apnea is a highly common condition that affects 30 million Americans. The CPAP machine is a longstanding, highly effective treatment for sleep apnea. Yet, many patients struggle to use the CPAP consistently because the mask, pressure, or equipment can feel uncomfortable or inconvenient. If this sounds like you, consider a sleep apnea mouth guard at Dr. Rose Magno‘s practice.

This oral appliance repositions the jaw to help keep the airway open, which can reduce symptoms and improve sleep quality. Many patients find it more comfortable and easier to use consistently than CPAP.

Why Consider a CPAP Alternative?

A CPAP is often considered the gold standard in sleep apnea treatment. It works by delivering pressurized air through a mask throughout the night. This helps improve oxygen flow during sleep. When used consistently, CPAP is highly effective at improving sleep quality and patients’ quality of life the next day. However, many individuals struggle with compliance. Patients often find CPAP awkward, bulky, or hard to tolerate. Sometimes they can’t tolerate the mask itself or the persistent air pressure. Patients who travel frequently find the device cumbersome.

These are just some reasons that patients end up failing to use the CPAP machine regularly. When they don’t comply with regular use, they miss out on the full benefits of treatment. In these cases, it’s time to consider a sleep apnea mouth guard.

How a Sleep Apnea Mouth Guard Works

When you first learn about the sleep apnea mouth guard, you might picture a sports mouth guard, but the two are actually quite different. An athletic bite guard helps to protect the teeth from wear or impact damage during activity. Instead, a sleep apnea oral appliance improves airflow by gently changing the shape and position of the jaw. These appliances are medically known as mandibular advancement devices. By moving the lower jaw forward, this device creates more space in the airway. This repositioning helps patients with mild to moderate sleep apnea.

To custom-craft your mouth guard, Dr. Magno takes impressions of your teeth and sends them to a dental lab. Once it arrives at the practice, Dr. Magno adjusts it for the ideal fit.

Many patients find the sleep apnea mouth guard more comfortable to wear each night. When adherence improves, people reap the benefits of a better night’s sleep in their day-to-day lives. The mouth guard is also smaller and more travel-friendly. Plus, unlike the CPAP machine, the oral appliance for sleep apnea is completely silent.

Still, this solution doesn’t always work for everyone. Patients with more severe sleep apnea often do best with CPAP, though you should still consider oral appliances if you can’t tolerate CPAP.
